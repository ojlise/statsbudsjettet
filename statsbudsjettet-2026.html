Norway plans to spend 2 164 billion kroner outside the petroleum sector in 2026 and to collect 1 712 billion in mainland revenue. The missing 452 billion — one krone in five — is transferred in from the oil fund. This page takes the Ministry of Finance's own tables and shows where every krone comes from, where it goes, and how the mix has moved since 2021.

How the budget balances

Two kinds of krone

Norway runs its state budget on a deliberate split. Everything the petroleum sector earns the state goes out of the budget and into the Government Pension Fund Global; a capped share comes back in to cover the gap between mainland revenue and spending. The budget is therefore always balanced on paper, and the real fiscal question is how big that transfer has become.

The number that actually binds. The structural non-oil deficit — the transfer stripped of business-cycle effects — is 579,4 mrd. kroner in 2026, or 2,8 % of the fund. The fiscal rule (handlingsregelen) sets an expected real return of 3 % as the long-run ceiling, so 2026 sits just under it. Measured against trend mainland GDP the deficit is 13,1 % — up 0,4 percentage points from 2025, and the highest share on record.

Spending side

Where the money goes

Four blocks account for two-thirds of the budget: the National Insurance Scheme (folketrygden) at 34 %, block grants to municipalities and counties at 12 %, the regional health trusts at 11 %, and defence — which jumped to 8,3 % in 2026 from 6,2 % a year earlier.

2021 → 2026

Five years of change

Non-petroleum spending rose 42,9 % in five years, 7,4 % a year. Mainland revenue rose faster still — 53,1 % — because 2021 was a pandemic-depressed tax year. Underneath those totals the composition moved sharply: defence nearly tripled, the electricity duty was cut by more than half, and interest on the state debt almost doubled.

The structural picture

The gap the fund fills keeps widening

Since 2022 mainland revenue has grown more slowly than spending, and the shaded gap — the non-oil deficit — has grown from 283 mrd. in 2022 to 452 mrd. in 2026. At the same time the petroleum cash flow financing it has fallen from its 2022 peak of 1 285 mrd. to 521 mrd.

Demography is a floor, not a trend

Old-age pensions reach 352,1 mrd. in 2026 with 1 098 000 recipients, 25 000 more than in 2025. Disability benefits (140,7) and work assessment allowance (56,6) are both growing faster than pensions; AAP recipients are projected at 171 900. This part of the budget is locked in by population, not by policy, and the government's own 2027–2029 projections put most of the future room for manoeuvre inside it.

Defence is on a plan, not a curve

The 2021→2026 defence CAGR of 22,6 % is an artefact of two things stacked on one line: the long-term defence plan, and 85 mrd. of Ukraine support through the Nansen programme (70 military, 15 civil), of which a temporary 50 mrd. increase is carried over from 2025. Extrapolating that rate is meaningless; read the long-term plan instead.

The fiscal rule caps the total

Spending growth is ultimately bounded by the 3 % expected real return on a 20 910 mrd. fund. The government puts underlying real spending growth at 1,0 % for 2026 — but realised growth has averaged 3,1 % a year since the 2022 budget, against 2,2 % over 2001–2019. Budgets are proposed lean and delivered rich; a forecast built on proposal-time growth rates will run low.

Definitions that change the answer
Oljekorrigert underskudd — non-oil deficit
Mainland revenue minus total spending, both excluding petroleum. 452,2 mrd. in 2026. It is covered krone for krone by the fund transfer, so the budget shows a zero balance.
Strukturelt, oljekorrigert underskudd — structural non-oil deficit
The same deficit adjusted for the business cycle (tax receipts, interest, unemployment benefits) and for accounting one-offs. 579,4 mrd. in 2026 = 2,8 % of the fund. This, not the raw deficit, is what the fiscal rule is measured against.
Saldert budsjett vs. regnskap
Saldert is the budget as finally adopted by the Storting in December; regnskap is what was actually spent. Supplementary appropriations during the year mean the accounts usually exceed the adopted budget, which is why the two bases are never mixed inside one chart here.
Utenom petroleumsvirksomhet og lånetransaksjoner
The scope used throughout: it strips out petroleum revenue and spending (which flow to the fund) and lending, share purchases and debt repayment (which are asset swaps, not resource use). Gross totals including those are 2 965 mrd. of spending in the adopted 2026 budget.
Underliggende, reell utgiftsvekst
Nominal spending growth less price growth, excluding petroleum spending, unemployment benefits and interest, and corrected for one-offs. 1,0 % for 2026 (21,5 mrd. 2026-kroner) against nominal growth of 4,1 % and price growth of 3,1 %.
